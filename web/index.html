The problem

The money is owed. You still have to chase it.

Under EU rules, a flight that lands three hours or more late owes each passenger €250 to €600, depending on distance. The UK and Canada have similar rules. But the airline only pays if you claim: find the form, prove the delay, wait. Every claim costs the passenger time, and claims firms keep a share of what they recover.

€250–600 per passenger

owed under EU Regulation 261/2004 for a 3 hour+ arrival delay, unless the cause was extraordinary, such as severe weather. Source

How it works

Two agents, one rule, one receipt.

The airline publishes its compensation rule once. After that, every late landing is handled by its payment agent, with a person approving each payout.

The passenger's agent sends the claim

Flight, date, booking and the wallet to pay. The booking reference never goes on chain, only a fingerprint of it.

The airline's agent checks the published rule

The rule lives in a contract on HSK Chain: for example 2 hours late pays $10, 3 hours $25, 5 hours $60. Code decides the amount, never an AI model.

It locks the booking before paying

A reservation on HSK Chain means a second claim for the same booking is refused, even if it arrives a second later.

A person approves, the agent pays through HSP

The airline sees who is paid, from where and how much, and approves. The agent then pays through HashKey Settlement Platform: order, quote, signature, submit, confirm.

The receipt is written on HSK Chain

The HSP transaction becomes the receipt. If a payment fails, the booking is released so it can be retried. If the result is unclear, it stays locked until it is resolved.

HSP

HashKey Settlement Platform

The airline's agent pays through HSP's agentic payment tools: an AP2-style cart, an x402 payment method and a signed mandate. HSP's gateway broadcasts the payment.

Policy and receipts

The rule, the reservation and the receipt live in one small contract on HSK Chain mainnet, so anyone can check what was paid and why.

Agent to agent

The passenger's agent sends terms, the airline's agent pays, and the passenger's agent checks the payment with HSP before showing "paid".

FAQ

Questions

Is this real money?

Not in the demo. HashKey Settlement Platform's hackathon environment settles on test networks with test USDC, and the demo amounts are small. The policy and receipts contract is on HSK Chain mainnet.

Are the flights real?

No. The demo uses simulated flights and you choose how late they land. A real airline would feed in its own operations data.

Who decides how much is paid?

The airline's published rule, read from the contract. The agent never invents an amount, and a person at the airline approves each payout before it is signed and again before it is sent.

What stops someone claiming twice?

Each booking is locked on HSK Chain before any payment starts. A second claim for the same flight and booking is refused, even with different spacing or capital letters.

What if a payment fails?

If HSP reports a failure, or the airline declines, the booking is released so it can be paid later. If the payment was sent but the result is unclear, the booking stays locked until HSP confirms either way.

Does my booking reference go on chain?

No. Only a one-way fingerprint of flight, date and booking is stored, plus the wallet that was paid and the amount.

Is the code open?

Yes, under the MIT licence: github.com/0xvikram/landed.
